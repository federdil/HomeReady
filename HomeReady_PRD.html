Section 1

Executive Summary

Buying a first home in the UK is one of the most financially consequential decisions a person will make — yet buyers navigate a fragmented, opaque, jargon-heavy process with almost no dedicated guidance. The average first-time buyer takes 12–18 months from initial research to completion and routinely encounters costly surprises at every stage: hidden fees, unreadable legal documents, and a conveyancing process that disappears into a black hole for months.

HomeReady is an AI-powered web application that acts as a knowledgeable friend throughout the entire first-time buyer journey — from initial affordability checks through to post-completion admin. It surfaces the right information at the right moment, translates complex documents into plain English, and gives buyers the confidence to make decisions without feeling lost or exploited.

There are approximately 400,000 first-time buyers in the UK each year (341K in 2024, up 19%). No single product owns the end-to-end journey. Existing tools (Rightmove, MoneyHelper, Which?) address individual moments but do not connect them into a coherent, personalised experience. The closest AI competitor (Jitty, launched March 2026) is still search and discovery only — HomeReady must own Stages 3–6 before they expand.

Stage 1 — Financial Readiness

PS-01
The true cost of buying is invisible
Buyers anchor on asking price and underestimate total acquisition cost by 10–15%. Stamp duty, solicitor fees, survey, broker, and moving costs are never presented together. The surprise typically hits at offer stage — when walking away is emotionally costly.
PS-02
Mortgage affordability is a black box
Buyers rely on rough rules of thumb (4.5× salary) without understanding how deposit size, credit history, or employment type shifts eligibility. Many begin searches outside their actual budget, wasting months and risking credit score damage from multiple hard searches.

Stage 2 — Property Evaluation

PS-03
Listing language obscures rather than informs
Estate agent listings are written to sell, not to inform. Euphemisms ("cosy", "investment opportunity", "vibrant area") consistently mislead. First-time buyers are most vulnerable, having no prior experience to calibrate against.
PS-04
Leasehold risk is a hidden trap
Short leases, high service charges, and ground rent clauses can make a property expensive to own and difficult to sell. Most buyers don't know what to look for and discover problems only after instructing a solicitor — when walking away is costly.
PS-05
Neighbourhood fit is assessed on gut feel
Commute time, noise, school quality, flood risk, and planned developments all affect liveability and resale value — but this data is scattered across dozens of sources and never synthesised per property.

Stage 3 — Offer & Negotiation

PS-06
Buyers don't know if the asking price is fair
Without comparable sales data in plain English, buyers either overpay out of fear or underbid unnecessarily. Overpaying by £5,000–£30,000 is common; underbidding loses the property entirely.
PS-07
Negotiation feels intimidating and improvised
Most first-time buyers have never negotiated a large purchase. Estate agents, who work for the seller, are the primary source of advice — a fundamental conflict of interest.

Stage 4 — Legal & Survey

PS-08
Conveyancing is a black hole
After offer acceptance, buyers enter a 3–6 month process with almost no proactive visibility. They don't know what is happening, what is blocking progress, or what actions they should be taking.
PS-09
Legal documents are written for lawyers
Draft contracts, title registers, and search results are dense legal documents. Buyers receive these and must either trust their solicitor entirely or pay expensive hourly rates for explanations.
PS-10
Survey results cause disproportionate panic
Without context, buyers either panic and pull out unnecessarily, or dismiss serious structural issues they should renegotiate on. There is no plain-English layer to help them interpret findings proportionally.

Stage 5 — Exchange & Completion

PS-11
The chain is opaque and fragile
Buyers have no visibility into chain health. Chain breaks are the single biggest cause of wasted time and money — costing buyers thousands in abortive legal and survey fees.
PS-12
Exchange to completion is poorly understood
The legal distinction between exchange and completion confuses most buyers. Obligations in this final window (buildings insurance from exchange, balance transfer, meter readings) are poorly communicated by any single party.

Stage 6 — Post-Completion

PS-13
Post-completion admin is an unexpected burden
SDLT payment (14-day deadline), Land Registry, utilities, electoral roll, insurance — no single party briefs buyers on these comprehensively. Things get missed, sometimes with financial or legal consequences.
PS-14
No framework for long-term ownership
Once buyers complete, support ends — yet critical decisions follow: when to remortgage, how to budget for maintenance, sinking fund obligations. Buyers revert to SVR (often 2–3% more expensive) through inaction.
Section 3

Goals

User Goals

  • G-U1Buyers arrive at each stage feeling informed and prepared, not overwhelmed.
  • G-U2Buyers avoid common financial mistakes through timely, contextual alerts.
  • G-U3Buyers understand every document they sign, with plain-English summaries surfacing the clauses that matter.
  • G-U4Buyers feel confident enough to negotiate and push back without paying for professional advice at every step.
  • G-U5Buyers complete their purchase without any critical admin tasks falling through the cracks.

Product Goals

  • G-P170% task completion rate across the six journey stages within 6 months of launch.
  • G-P230% reduction in user-reported stress at offer and exchange milestones vs. baseline.
  • G-P340% of new users through word-of-mouth referral within 12 months.
  • G-P4Establish HomeReady as the primary reference product in first-time buyer communities (Reddit, MSE) within 18 months.
Section 4

Non-Goals (v1)

  • Renter journey support — distinct problem set, separate future initiative.
  • Mortgage brokerage or financial advice — HomeReady surfaces context and flags options but will not provide regulated FCA advice or act as a lender intermediary.
  • Solicitor or surveyor marketplace — referral fees and SRA compliance are out of scope.
  • New-build purchases — distinct process (reservation fees, Help to Buy, snagging) warrants separate product treatment.
  • Buy-to-let or second properties — exclusively for owner-occupier first-time buyers.
  • Mobile app (iOS/Android) — v1 is a responsive web app; native apps considered in v2 based on usage data.

Section 7

Open Questions

Blocking — Must Resolve Before Launch

Legal
Does providing plain-English summaries of legal documents constitute regulated legal advice under the Legal Services Act 2007? Does a prominent disclaimer suffice, or is a legal review required before launch?
Legal
Does generating a negotiation script or affordability estimate trigger FCA financial promotion rules? Requires legal counsel review before these features are publicly promoted.
Engineering
Which data source will power comparable price analysis (R-08)? Land Registry data has a 3–6 month publication lag. Is this acceptable for v1, or do we need a paid alternative?

Non-Blocking — Resolve During Build

Product
Should users be able to share their HomeReady dashboard with a partner or family member? If so, what is the collaboration model (view-only vs. edit access)?
Product
What is the monetisation model? Freemium with paywalled document analysis, one-time purchase, or subscription? This affects v1 feature gate decisions.
Design
How do we handle users who sign up mid-journey (e.g. already at offer stage)? Onboarding must accommodate entry from Stage 1 through Stage 4.

Agentic Architecture — Neighbourhood Agent

The Neighbourhood Intelligence Agent uses Claude's native tool-use capability in an agentic loop. Claude autonomously decides which tools to call (TfL transport, Environment Agency flood risk, Ofsted schools, web search) based on the postcode and buyer priorities, then synthesises multi-source results into a structured briefing. Tool-call progress is streamed to the client in real time via SSE, eliminating perceived latency for an 8–15 second operation.
